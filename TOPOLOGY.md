# Rogue: The Millenium Glitch — the Topology of Escape (difficulty as distance × borders)

The game is ONE map of souls, all moving toward Nanjing. The win condition
for each is the same: reach Nanjing. The DIFFICULTY is not a number — it is
topology: how far, and whether the world will let them move.

## The movement law (the spine)
No planes. Only:
  - their own feet
  - bicycles
  - horses
  - cars
  - trains
  - ships

The whole playable network IS the topology: distance matters only if a line
of travel joins two points, and what that line is (road / rail / sea / fence
/ checkpoint / mountain / closed border) decides how far a soul truly is.

## The souls (difficulty settings, to be detailed as we go)
1.  **Octavia** — homeless, New York.
    *Difficulty = distance.* Rich ground, dense street, many routes. She is
    the gentle open — the furthest road, but the most walkable.
    *Victory:* trans, she must cross into "progressive" Spain to learn that
    even there she is not safe — and realizes **China is the only place that
    may let her live.** The journey is a migration toward the one shore that
    does not turn her away.

2.  **Ahmad** — a refugee camp in the Western Sahara.
    *Difficulty = containment, not distance.* Fences, checkpoints, no road
    out. Hard because the topology is a cage.
    *Victory:* Ahmad's solace is **killing an Arabian prince** — the figure
    he holds responsible for the cage and the misery. This is done through
    **dialogue, not combat**: a confrontation where words become the weapon
    that ends him. A revenge carried over the whole journey.

3.  **Rafael** — trying to escape Gaza.
    *Difficulty = the hardest topology.* A sealed wall and closed borders;
    nowhere to walk. Escape is not a road — it is a hole. (The hardest start.)

4.  **Vasile** — running from Botoșani, Romania.
    *Difficulty = emigration by land.* A journey through a Europe of border
    regimes; foot, bicycle, and rail; cold; documents; weather.
    *Victory:* similar to Ervin's — a choice of end, return or roam — but
    Vasile **wanders more**, less anchored, restless by nature.

5.  **Ervin** — moving from Xinjiang to work in Nanjing.
    *Difficulty = the reverse journey.* Not fleeing but being moved to the
    very point of convergence. Geographically the shortest — but the path
    least under his own control.
    *Victory:* **either return home to Xinjiang, or continue and explore the
    wider world.** The convergence is a fork, not a finish.

## The ending: meeting your love
The story does not have to end at the destination. **A soul may end their
story when they meet their love** — when one of the people they meet along a
road stops being a stranger and becomes the person they choose to stay for.

- It is a *can*, not a *must*: the soul may instead go on with their partner,
  walking on toward their other victories (once multiplayer lets two hearts
  travel the same road).
- This is a **second way to end**, coexisting with the per-soul victories
  (Ahmad's prince, Octavia's shore). For souls whose fixed end is a revenge
  or a refuge, meeting a love is another door — often the hardest one,
  because *choosing to stop wandering* is the last act of courage.
- Thematically it is the true finish line: the places are settings; the real
  end is a person.

## Convergence
All five roads end in Nanjing. The five souls do not have to be separate
games — they can share the one map, cross paths, maybe even help or fail each
other. (Design openness: decide later whether runs are per-soul or a shared
web.)

## NPCs in the world
Not every life is a walker. Some are the people they meet.

- **Mau** — a curtain-maker's son. Through helping his family's trade, he
  ends up in Shanghai (note: Shanghai, not Nanjing). An NPC the walkers can
  meet — a life formed not by flight but by craft and care for kin. (The
  user expects to add more NPCs over time.)

## The core that stays true for every soul
- No combat. The difficulty is surviving and moving through a world built
  from languages, borders, weather, and distance.
- The drone / saviours motif stays for Octavia; the other souls get their own
  watchful presences or none.
- Permadeath. One run. The world differs every time.
- A "good" run is a complete crossing in a bounded time.
